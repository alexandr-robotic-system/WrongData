05 / SCENARIOS

Eight situations. One set of rules.

Two short simulations show how the system behaves on a real site — from a routine patrol to an intrusion with people inside the perimeter, a guard on the ground and ground robots joining the response. Every step below is a rule the fleet follows, not a scripted animation.

SIMULATION   These films are system-logic simulations of the target behaviour. See status & roadmap for what is flight-tested today.

PART 1 / AUTONOMOUS PERIMETER DEFENSE

A perimeter that patrols itself and answers an intrusion.

CASE 01

Protected zone & patrol

A perimeter is defined as a closed route of N points — the patrol starts and ends at P1. N drones are assigned to the zone; the one with the highest charge flies the route every N minutes at a set height above terrain, filming the whole way. Landing and the next takeoff happen side by side on the port.

  • RULE 5.0.1Highest-charge drone patrols; drones below the battery floor stay grounded.
  • RULE 5.1On intrusion, 1–2 drones launch in queue order with safe takeoff separation.
CASE 02

Intrusion & escort

A target crosses the perimeter. The AI flags it, drones launch, lock on and escort the intruder to the boundary while streaming video to the operator.

  1. Intrusion detected at the boundary; the event goes to the fleet.
  2. 1–2 drones launch in queue order and lock onto the target.
  3. Base drones join and follow the escort rule — the target stays covered to the border.
  4. 30 seconds with no new intrusion: drones return, land and are ready for the next patrol.
CASE 03

Battery handover

A drone on escort runs low. It cannot simply leave — the target must stay observed. It requests a replacement; a fresh drone takes over the escort, and only then does the first one land and charge. Coverage is never lost while drones rotate.

  • RULE 5.2At least one drone stays on patrol or on the target at all times.
  • RULE 5.3Low battery → request replacement → hand over → go home.

PART 2 / PEOPLE, GUARDS AND ROBOTS

People inside, a guard on the ground, robots in the loop.

CASE 04

People inside the perimeter

Three persons are detected inside the zone. The alarm chain runs drone → base → rapid response post. One or two drones are assigned per person; when the group splits up, so do the drones. Live feeds go straight to the response post, and every target stays tracked until a guard arrives.

CASE 05

Guard watch

A guard heads out with three drones bound to his watch. They follow him in formation wherever he goes. One command from the wrist — 2 forward, 1 cover — sends two drones ahead to the target while one stays over the guard.

  1. Guard leaves the post; W1–W3 follow in formation.
  2. Watch command: two drones forward to the target, one covers the guard.
  3. Face captured and sent to the response post — the intruder is identified before the guard gets close.
CASE 06

Non-lethal deterrence

Spotlight and a voice warning: "Stop. This is a protected area. Security is on the way." The intruder is held in place by light and sound until the guard arrives — no contact, no escalation.

CASE 07

Ground robots on the same watch

Ground robots take orders from the same wrist interface. UGV-1: come to me. UGV-2: take the high point — it holds the highest position with its mast camera up, giving overwatch of the whole area for drones and guards alike.

CASE 08

Delivery on demand

The guard picks what he needs on the watch — handcuffs or a med kit — and UGV-1 delivers it to his position. Aerial and ground units share one task queue and one map.

Aerial coverage, ground response and a human in the loop — the same rules, the same map, the same task queue. That is what "one system" means.
