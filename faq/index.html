08 / FAQ

Questions we get before the first meeting.

Short, direct answers. If yours isn't here, one email gets it answered within two working days.

08
How far can the drones fly from the port?

A 7-inch quadcopter in our configuration covers a patrol of several kilometres on one charge. In practice a port covers a radius of roughly 1–2 km with a comfortable reserve, which is why large sites get more ports rather than longer flights. The limiting factor today is the radio link, which we are extending — see the status table.

Does it work at night and in bad weather?

Night is where the system is most useful: patrols run on schedule, and cameras with illumination or thermal sensors see what a guard cannot. Rain, wind and temperature have limits set per drone; when they are exceeded, a scheduled patrol is skipped and logged rather than forced. Snow and fog reduce camera usefulness; we say so in the assessment rather than promise otherwise.

How many drones does a site need?

One group of three with one port covers a typical warehouse, farm compound or gated community. The number grows with area, with the number of events you expect at the same time, and with how fast you want a response. The assessment gives you a number and the reasoning behind it.

What happens if the link to the drone is lost?

The drone returns to the port by itself along a pre-flown route and lands. Returning home does not depend on the link or the internet — it is a flight-controller failsafe. The port and the cloud log the event and alert your contact.

Can it be integrated with the alarm system we already have?

Yes — that is the normal case. An alarm, a camera analytics event or a gate sensor becomes a mission trigger; the drones go first, stream video, and your operator decides what to do. We integrate with dry-contact outputs, IP alarms and common camera platforms; the assessment lists what your system exposes.

Who sees the footage, and where is it stored?

You do. Footage and mission logs belong to the client; we keep them for the retention period agreed in the contract and delete them afterwards. Routes exclude public roads and neighbouring property. We access the data only for support, with your consent.

Is it legal to fly drones over my site in Moldova?

In most cases yes, with conditions: outside controlled airspace or with coordination, with registered and marked drones, within a geofenced area over your own property. We check the airspace for every site before the pilot and obtain what is required. See flying legally, by design.

Do I need a pilot or an operator?

No pilot. Someone on site needs to receive alerts and act on them — a guard, a manager or your monitoring desk. The system flies, lands, charges and reports by itself; we monitor fleet health remotely.

How long does it take to get started?

A site assessment takes one to two weeks and gives you a written plan. A pilot — one group and one port on your site — runs four to eight weeks. By the end of it you know what the system sees, what it costs to run, and whether it should grow.

How is it priced?

A fixed fee for the assessment, a fixed fee for the pilot, and then a monthly service fee per port that covers operation, monitoring, maintenance and hardware replacement. We don't publish a price list because sites differ too much; the assessment ends with a quote.

Is the product finished?

No, and we say so on the status page. The autonomous flight stack is flight-tested; the port, the group logic and the long-range link are being built; the AI and guard-interface features are concepts. Pilots are for sites that want to shape the product, not buy a finished one.

What do you need from me to give a first answer?

Three lines: what kind of site, roughly how big or how long the perimeter is, and what you want the system to do first. Send them to alexandr@wrongdata.md or use the contact page.
