01 / THE PROBLEM

Manual drone response doesn't scale.

A camera can record an event. An alarm can raise a signal. But a conventional drone still needs a person — to retrieve it, prepare it, fly it, bring it back and recharge it. That's slow, depends on an operator, and breaks down across large sites.

WRONG DATA turns the drone into an autonomous part of a site's infrastructure. Once configured, the client doesn't need to be a pilot — they get the result: video, status, route, alert and report.

04 / AUTONOMOUS DRONE FLEET

Nine drones. Three independent groups.

Each group runs one lead drone and two follower drones. The lead receives the mission, reads the situation, coordinates the group and reallocates resources. Followers patrol, monitor from separate angles, or handle sub-tasks. Every drone can operate alone — but the system's strength is coordinated group behaviour.

LEAD DRONE

Receives the mission, analyses the situation, coordinates the group and reallocates resources.

FOLLOWER DRONE

Patrols, monitors from a different angle, or carries out a separate sub-task alongside the lead.

FOLLOWER DRONE

Works in parallel with the other follower, keeping coverage on more than one point at once.

SCENARIO / TWO EVENTS, ONE SYSTEM

  1. An alarm or the AI detects an intrusion at the site boundary.
  2. A group of three drones launches from the Drone Port.
  3. The lead drone arrives and splits the area into observation sectors.
  4. One or two followers stay on the first event, streaming video.
  5. A second event appears — movement at the gate, or elsewhere on site.
  6. The lead drone moves to the second event.
  7. The system decides whether one follower is enough, or a second group should launch.
  8. Coverage of the first zone is never dropped; both events stay observed.

This isn't a swarm for show. It's a logic for allocating autonomous resources across more than one task at a time.
