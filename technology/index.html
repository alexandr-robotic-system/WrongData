How the parts connect.

Four layers, each with its own job. The drone flies even if the cloud is down; the port recovers the drone even if the operator is away; the client gets the result without touching any of it.

What happens when something fails.

Autonomy is only useful if it is boring when things go wrong. Every drone carries the same failsafe configuration; every rule below is a flight-controller setting, not a promise.

FailureBehaviour
Radio link lostThe drone finishes the current waypoint, then returns to the port along a pre-flown route and lands. No link is needed to come home.
Battery lowWarning level triggers a handover request to the group; critical level triggers an unconditional return-to-launch.
GNSS / RTK degradedPrecision landing waits for a solid fix; if none comes, the drone lands on the pad's visual marker (AprilTag) and reports a degraded landing.
Geofence breachedA hard fence around the site stops the drone at the boundary and brings it back — it cannot be flown outside the permitted area.
Port unavailableEach site has a defined alternate landing spot; the drone lands there and the system alerts the on-site contact.
Weather out of limitsWind, rain and temperature limits are checked before every launch; scheduled patrols are skipped and logged, not forced.
